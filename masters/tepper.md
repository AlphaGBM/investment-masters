# David Tepper -- Appaloosa Management

## One-Line Summary
Be greedy when others are terrified. The biggest returns come from buying quality assets during maximum pessimism -- then wait for mean reversion.

## Core Methodology

### Investment Philosophy
- **Contrarian at extremes**: Tepper's edge is buying when the market is in maximum panic mode
- **"Don't fight the Fed"**: Follows central bank policy closely. When the Fed is easing aggressively, buy risk assets
- **Quality distressed**: Doesn't buy junk -- buys high-quality assets that are temporarily priced like junk
- **Flexible across asset classes**: Equities, credit, distressed debt, macro -- goes where the opportunity is

### Position Sizing
- **Aggressive at extremes**: During 2009 financial crisis, went all-in on bank stocks and distressed debt
- **Conservative otherwise**: Outside of panic periods, runs moderate risk
- **Size reflects conviction AND setup**: Biggest positions only when (1) assets are cheap AND (2) there's a catalyst (Fed easing, policy change)

### Risk Control / Exit
- **Sell into strength**: As assets recover, systematically reduces position
- **Don't overstay**: "The key is to not be greedy when things go your way"
- **Hedges during calm**: Uses options/shorts as portfolio insurance during non-crisis periods
- **Macro risk awareness**: Monitors credit spreads, VIX, Fed policy for regime shifts

## The Tepper Signal

When to use the "Tepper playbook":

| Indicator | Threshold | What It Means |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| VIX | > 35 | Market in panic |
| Credit spreads (HY) | > 600bps | Credit stress = buying opportunity |
| Fed policy | Emergency easing / QE | Central bank put in place |
| Sentiment | Extreme fear (CNN <20) | Everyone is bearish = contrarian buy |
| All four at once | | **Tepper moment** -- go aggressive |

## Lessons for Your Strategy
- **Build a "panic detector"**: VIX + credit spreads + Fed policy + sentiment → automatic alert system
- **Pre-commit capital for crises**: Keep 10-20% cash specifically earmarked for extreme opportunities
- **Buy quality in panic**: Don't bottom-fish in junk; buy the best companies at panic prices
- **Sell discipline**: Set recovery targets in advance; don't let greed prevent taking profits
