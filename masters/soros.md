# George Soros -- Soros Fund Management

## One-Line Summary
Markets are driven by reflexivity -- perceptions change fundamentals, which change perceptions. Find the feedback loop, ride it, and get out before it reverses.

## Core Methodology

### Investment Philosophy
- **Reflexivity**: Markets are NOT efficient. Participants' biased views influence fundamentals, which in turn reinforce biased views -- creating boom-bust cycles
- **Macro first**: Top-down thinker. Identifies macro imbalances (currencies, rates, credit) then finds the trade
- **Thesis → Test → Scale**: Form a thesis, take a small position to test it, then scale aggressively if confirmed
- **"Invest first, investigate after"**: Act on conviction, then validate. If wrong, exit immediately

### Position Sizing
- **Asymmetric conviction scaling**: Start small. If the market confirms your thesis, scale up dramatically
- **Go for the jugular**: When you're right AND the market is wrong, size up massively (1992 GBP trade was $10B+)
- **No half measures**: A great trade with small size is a wasted opportunity
- **Back hurts, reduce**: Famous quote -- when his back literally hurt from stress, he knew the position was too big

### Risk Control / Exit
- **Survival first**: "It's not whether you're right or wrong that matters, but how much money you make when you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong"
- **Quick exits on broken thesis**: Zero ego about being wrong. Reverses positions within hours if thesis breaks
- **Pain as a signal**: Physical stress = position too large or thesis weakening
- **Reflexivity works both ways**: The same feedback loop that creates the boom creates the bust -- exit before the reversal

## Lessons for Your Strategy
- **Look for reflexive feedback loops**: Where is perception driving reality which drives more perception?
- **Start small, scale into winners**: Don't put full position on day one
- **Cut losses with zero ego**: Being wrong is fine; staying wrong is fatal
- **Macro context matters**: Even the best stock pick fails in a hostile macro environment
