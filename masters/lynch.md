# Peter Lynch -- Fidelity Magellan Fund

## One-Line Summary
Invest in what you know, do the homework, and find "ten-baggers" early -- growth at a reasonable price, one well-understood company at a time.

## Core Methodology

### Stock Selection
- **"Invest in what you know"**: Edge comes from everyday observation -- products and trends you notice before Wall Street does
- **Six categories**: Slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds, and asset plays -- each judged by different rules
- **GARP / PEG**: Growth at a reasonable price; a PEG ratio (P/E ÷ earnings growth) at or below 1 is attractive
- **Do the homework**: "Know what you own, and know why you own it" -- read the financials, understand the story
- **Let winners run**: A handful of ten-baggers outweighs many small losers

### Position Sizing
- **Broadly diversified**: Magellan held hundreds (at times over 1,000) of names -- the opposite of Buffett-style concentration
- Add to winners as the story improves; "don't pull the flowers and water the weeds"

### Risk Control / Exit
- **Sell when the story changes, not when the price moves**: thesis deterioration, category shift, or fundamentals breaking
- **Ignore macro forecasting**: "If you spend 13 minutes a year on economics, you've wasted 10 minutes"
- Rotate within categories as growth and valuation normalize

## Track Record (not a current 13F filer)
Lynch ran Fidelity's Magellan Fund from 1977 to 1990, compounding roughly 29% per year and growing assets from about $18M to ~$14B. He retired from active management in 1990, so there is no current 13F to track -- his methodology lives in his books and the Magellan record.

## Recent Views
- Long retired; his enduring message is that ordinary investors can beat Wall Street by leveraging firsthand knowledge and patience
- "The real key to making money in stocks is not to get scared out of them"

## Lessons for Your Strategy
- **PEG over raw P/E**: Pay for growth, but only at a reasonable multiple
- **Categorize before you judge**: A cyclical and a stalwart need completely different playbooks
- **Let winners compound**: Don't trim a great business just because it's up

## AlphaGBM Systematic Equivalent
- GARP screen: PEG <= 1, consistent earnings growth, manageable debt
- Category tagging: classify each ticker (stalwart / fast grower / cyclical / turnaround) to apply the right ruleset
- "Story break" exit: flag when growth or margins diverge from the original thesis
